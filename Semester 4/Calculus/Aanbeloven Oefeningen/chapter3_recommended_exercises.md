# Chapter 3 — Recommended Exercises for the Oefeningen Exam

This file is a practical Chapter 3 study guide based on the **course exercise sets (3A, 3B, 3C, 3D, 3E, 3F, 3G, 3H)** and the **old oefeningen exams** you uploaded.

The goal is not to do everything, but to do the exercises that are **most similar to the recurring exam patterns**.

---

## Big picture

For Chapter 3, the oefeningen exam keeps coming back to a fairly small group of patterns:

1. **Derivative calculations** of tricky functions
2. **Higher derivatives** such as second or third derivatives
3. **Full function investigations** of rational functions, including asymptotes
4. **Polar curve investigations** with a sign table and sketch
5. **Convexity / concavity / inflection points**
6. **Occasional mean value theorem / Lagrange questions**

So the best strategy is:

- first master **3H**
- then do the strongest parts of **3G**
- then make sure you are solid in **3A** and **3B**
- then do the most useful parts of **3D**
- use **3F** as support for asymptotes inside function investigations
- leave **3E** for last

# Priority 1 — Absolutely do these

## Oefeningenreeks 3H

This is one of the most exam-like Chapter 3 blocks of all.

### Must do

- **3H.5.1–3H.5.4**
- **3H.8.1–3H.8.5**

Why these matter:
- These are the closest to the recurring exam questions where you must make a **tekenonderzoek of a polar curve up to the first derivative** and then draw it.
- This pattern comes back again and again: period, zeros, symmetry, first derivative, extrema of `r(\theta)`, then sketch.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2018–2019, 1st sit, Q6**
- **2019–2020, 1st sit, Q3**
- **2020–2021, 1st sit, Q3**
- **2021–2022, 1st sit, Q6**
- **2022–2023, 1st sit, Q6**
- **2023–2024, 2nd sit, Q6**
- **2024–2025, 1st sit, Q6**

### Also strongly recommended

- **3H.6.1–3H.6.4**
- **3H.7.1–3H.7.8**
- **3H.3.1–3H.3.3**
- **3H.4.1–3H.4.4**

Why:
- These teach you how to convert between cartesian and polar form and how to recognize the standard shapes fast.
- That makes the real exam-style polar investigations much easier.

## Oefeningenreeks 3G

This is the other very high-yield block, especially for the full graph-study questions.

### Must do

- **3G.9.1–3G.9.8**
- **3G.11.1–3G.11.9**

Why:
- These are the closest to the recurring exam questions where you must do a **full function investigation** of a rational or homographic function.
- They train exactly the things that keep returning: domain, zeros, poles, asymptotes, first derivative, second derivative, monotonicity, extrema, concavity, and sketch.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2018–2019, 2nd sit, Q6**
- **2021–2022, 2nd sit, Q6**
- **2022–2023, 2nd sit, Q6**
- **2023–2024, 1st sit, Q6**
- **2024–2025, 2nd sit, Q6**

### Strongly recommended

- **3G.1–3G.5**
- **3G.8**
- **3G.10**

Why:
- These train polynomial and rational graph studies, parameter questions, and inequality interpretation from the graph.
- They are very close to the harder exam questions where you must combine graph study with extra reasoning.

### Also recommended

- **3G.12.1–3G.12.10**
- **3G.13.1–3G.13.8**
- **3G.14.1–3G.14.2**

Why:
- These are very useful for the trigonometric / cyclometric function investigation style.
- They are especially relevant because the Chapter 3 model questions also contain full trig graph studies.

# Priority 2 — Very high yield

## Oefeningenreeks 3A

This block is the main source for the recurring “calculate the derivative” question.

### Must do

- **3A.10.1–3A.10.11**
- **3A.11.1–3A.11.6**
- **3A.17.1–3A.17.6**

Why:
- These are the most exam-like derivative drills: products, quotients, trig, and more complicated compositions.
- Many old exams contain exactly one question of this type.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2018–2019, 1st sit, Q5**
- **2021–2022, 1st sit, Q5**
- **2021–2022, 2nd sit, Q5**
- **2023–2024, 1st sit, Q5**
- **2024–2025, 1st sit, Q5**

### Strongly recommended

- **3A.8.20–3A.8.25**
- **3A.9.1–3A.9.10**
- **3A.15**
- **3A.16.1–3A.16.2**

Why:
- These build the algebra you need before the harder quotient/composition questions.
- They also help with tangents and special-point derivatives, which are useful inside graph studies.

## Oefeningenreeks 3B

This is the highest-yield support block for second derivatives, third derivatives, and repeated differentiation.

### Must do

- **3B.1.9–3B.1.15**
- **3B.2.1–3B.2.8**
- **3B.4.1–3B.4.6**

Why:
- Old exams very often ask for a **second derivative** or **third derivative**, not just a first derivative.
- This block is the closest match to those recurring questions.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2019–2020, 2nd sit, Q3**
- **2022–2023, 2nd sit, Q5**
- **2023–2024, 2nd sit, Q5**
- **2024–2025, 2nd sit, Q5**

### Useful support exercises

- **3B.1.1–3B.1.8**
- **3B.3**
- **3B.5**
- **3B.6.1–3B.6.6**

Why:
- These strengthen technique and theory.
- The `3B.6` family is more theoretical and less directly exam-like, so I would do it after the derivative blocks above.

# Priority 3 — Important after the above

## Oefeningenreeks 3D

This block becomes very useful once you already know how to differentiate well.

### Must do

- **3D.2.1–3D.2.10**
- **3D.8.1–3D.8.8**

Why:
- `3D.2` is the core block for increasing/decreasing and local extrema.
- `3D.8` is very important for convexity, concavity, inflection points, and inflection tangents.
- Those are directly tied to the Chapter 3 graph-study questions.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2022–2023, 1st sit, Q5**
- also many full function investigations where these ideas are required even if not asked separately

### Also recommended

- **3D.3**
- **3D.4**
- **3D.5**
- **3D.6**
- **3D.7.1–3D.7.5**

Why:
- These train parameter conditions for extrema and harder extrema questions.
- They are excellent preparation for tougher polynomial and rational graph problems.

---

## Oefeningenreeks 3F

This block is not always asked as a standalone question, but it is essential inside full function investigations.

### Strongly recommended

- **3F.1.1–3F.1.14**
- **3F.2.1–3F.2.7**

Why:
- These train vertical, horizontal, and oblique asymptotes, plus the position of the graph relative to them.
- That is exactly what you need for the recurring full investigation questions from `3G` and the old exams.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- especially the recurring full graph-study questions:
  - **2021–2022, 2nd sit, Q6**
  - **2022–2023, 2nd sit, Q6**
  - **2023–2024, 1st sit, Q6**
  - **2024–2025, 2nd sit, Q6**

### Also recommended

- **3F.3**
- **3F.4**
- **3F.5**

Why:
- These are useful for understanding asymptotes in more general parameterized settings.
- They are less direct than `3F.1–2`, but still very good practice.

# Priority 4 — Useful, but less often copied directly

## Oefeningenreeks 3C

This block is about Rolle, Lagrange, and related theorem questions.

### Strongly recommended

- **3C.1–3C.7**
- **3C.13–3C.15**

Why:
- There are occasional exam questions where you must use the mean value theorem or explain why it applies or does not apply.
- `3C.4–7` are especially practical and exam-like.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2018–2019, 2nd sit, Q5**
- plus some model Chapter 3 questions about tangents parallel to chords

### Lower priority inside 3C

- **3C.8–3C.12**
- **3C.16.1–3C.16.7**

Why:
- These are still useful, but they are not as repeatedly mirrored in the old oefeningen exams as the derivative / function-study / polar blocks.

# Final advice

If your goal is pure exam efficiency, do **not** spread your time equally over all of Chapter 3.

The highest return comes from mastering:

- **polar curve studies from 3H**
- **full function investigations from 3G**
- **derivative and higher-derivative drills from 3A and 3B**
- **extrema / convexity tools from 3D**
- **asymptotes from 3F**

That is where the old exams keep coming back.
