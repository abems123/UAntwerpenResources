# Chapter 2 — Recommended Exercises for the Oefeningen Exam

This file is a practical Chapter 2 study guide based on the **course exercise sets (2A, 2B, 2C, 2D, 2E, 2F)** and the **old oefeningen exams** you uploaded.

The goal is not to do everything, but to do the exercises that are **most similar to the recurring exam patterns**.

---

## Big picture

For Chapter 2, the oefeningen exam keeps coming back to just a few patterns:

1. **Limits without l'Hôpital**
2. **Exponential equations**
3. **Logarithmic equations**
4. **Inverse trig / arctan identities and proofs**

That means the highest-return blocks are not 2A or 2F. They are mainly:

- **2D** for limits
- **2E** for exponential/logarithmic limits and equations
- **2B** for the recurring arctan / inverse trig proof style

---

# Priority 1 — Absolutely do these

## Oefeningenreeks 2D

This is the most exam-like limit block of Chapter 2.

### Must do

- **2D.1.1–2D.1.23**

Why these matter:
- They are the closest to the recurring exam question where you get several limits and must solve them **without l'Hôpital**.
- They include exactly the kind of trig, root, rational, and inverse-trig endpoint limits that keep showing up.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2018–2019, 2nd sit, Q3**
- **2019–2020, 1st sit, Q2**
- **2019–2020, 2nd sit, Q2**
- **2021–2022, 1st sit, Q4**
- **2021–2022, 2nd sit, Q4**
- **2022–2023, 1st sit, Q4**
- **2022–2023, 2nd sit, Q4**
- **2023–2024, 1st sit, Q4**
- **2023–2024, 2nd sit, Q4**
- **2024–2025, 1st sit, Q4**
- **2024–2025, 2nd sit, Q4**

### Also strongly recommended

- **2D.6.1–2D.6.9**
- **2D.8.1–2D.8.15**

Why:
- These are the more full exam style rational, root, and asymptotic limit drills.
- They train the exact algebraic manipulation style you need when the exam limit is not just a basic trig limit.

## Oefeningenreeks 2E

This is the second most important block, because Chapter 2 exam questions very often ask for an **exponential** or **logarithmic** equation, and 2E is exactly that block.

### Must do for exponential equations

- **2E.8.1–2E.8.22**
- **2E.10.1–2E.10.27**

Why:
- These are the exact “solve the exponential equation” drills.
- Many old exams have one full question of this type.
- The substitution style from `2E.10` is especially important.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2018–2019, 1st sit, Q3**
- **2021–2022, 2nd sit, Q3**
- **2022–2023, 1st sit, Q3**
- **2023–2024, 1st sit, Q3**
- **2024–2025, 1st sit, Q3**
- also the **model Chapter 2 exam, Q2**

### Must do for logarithmic equations

- **2E.18.1–2E.18.10**
- **2E.26.1–2E.26.13**
- **2E.26.14**
- **2E.26.18**
- **2E.26.19**
- **2E.26.31**
- **2E.26.39**

Why:
- These are the closest to the recurring exam style where you solve a log equation exactly and must be careful with the domain.
- **2E.26.39** is especially exam-like.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2020–2021, 1st sit, Q2**
- **2023–2024, 2nd sit, Q3**
- **2024–2025, 2nd sit, Q3**
- also the **model Chapter 2 exam, Q4**

### Must do for exp/log limits

- **2E.1**
- **2E.3.1–2E.3.4**
- **2E.4.1–2E.4.22**
- **2E.7.1–2E.7.5**

Why:
- These train the classic `e`-limits and `ln(1+x)` limits that often feed directly into the Chapter 2 limit question.

# Priority 2 — Very high yield

## Oefeningenreeks 2B

This is the inverse trig block: `Bgsin`, `Bgcos`, `Bgtan`, `Bgcot`.

### Must do

- **2B.4.1–2B.4.8**
- **2B.5.1–2B.5.4**
- **2B.6.1–2B.6.12**

Why:
- This is the most exam-like part of 2B.
- Old exams repeatedly ask you to prove that a sum of inverse tangents equals some clean constant such as `\dfrac{\pi}{4}`.
- If there is one subblock from 2B you really should not skip, it is **2B.6**.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2018–2019, 2nd sit, Q4**
- **2021–2022, 1st sit, Q3**
- **2022–2023, 2nd sit, Q3**
- also the **model Chapter 2 exam, Q3**

### Also strongly recommended

- **2B.7**
- **2B.8.1–2B.8.4**
- **2B.9.1–2B.9.4**
- **2B.10**
- **2B.11**

Why:
- These strengthen the exact identity manipulations used in the proof questions.
- **2B.7** is especially useful because it is close to the old exam style where an inverse trig sum has to be shown equal to a constant.

# Priority 3 — Useful support

## Oefeningenreeks 2C

This block is about continuous extension, one-sided limits, limits at infinity, and the formal language of limits.

### Strongly recommended

- **2C.1.1–2C.1.9**
- **2C.2**
- **2C.3–2C.8**

Why:
- These help you recognize removable discontinuities, vertical asymptotes, one-sided limits, and infinite limits.
- They are not as directly copied as 2D, but they make 2D much easier.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2018–2019, 1st sit, Q4**
- **2019–2020, 1st sit, Q2**
- support for almost all later limit questions

### Lower priority inside 2C

- **2C.9–2C.10**

Why:
- These are more about formal limit definitions.
- Good for theory and precision, but less directly repeated than the calculation-heavy blocks.

---

# Priority 4 — Do only after the above

## Oefeningenreeks 2A

This block is mainly domain, image, continuity, discontinuity, and continuity theorems.

### Useful parts

- **2A.8**
- **2A.11–2A.12**
- **2A.13**

Why:
- These help with basic continuity/discontinuity intuition.
- But compared with 2D, 2E, and 2B, they are much less represented in the oefeningen exams.

### Low priority inside 2A

- most of **2A.1–2A.7**
- **2A.15–2A.18**

Why:
- Important for understanding the chapter,
- but not the best use of time if your goal is old-exam efficiency.

---

# Low priority

## Oefeningenreeks 2F

This is the hyperbolic block: `sinh`, `cosh`, `tanh`, inverse hyperbolics, and related identities.

### Only do if you have extra time

- **2F.1–2F.4**

Why:
- They are nice exercises and useful mathematically,
- but they are **not** among the recurring Chapter 2 oefeningen exam patterns I found.

So for pure exam efficiency, **2F is last**.

# Final advice

For Chapter 2, the biggest trap is to spread your time too evenly.

The best return is:

- **limits from 2D**
- **exp/log limits and equations from 2E**
- **inverse trig proofs from 2B**

That is where the old oefeningen exams keep coming back.
