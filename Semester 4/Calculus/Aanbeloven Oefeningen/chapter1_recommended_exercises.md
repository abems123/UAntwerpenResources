# Chapter 1 — Recommended Exercises for the Oefeningen Exam

This file is a practical Chapter 1 study guide based on the **course exercise sets (1B, 1C, 1D, 1E)** and the **old oefeningen exams** you uploaded.

The goal is not to do everything, but to do the exercises that are **most similar to the recurring exam patterns**.

---

## Big picture

For Chapter 1, the oefeningen exam keeps coming back to a fairly small group of patterns:

1. **Complex arithmetic** and writing answers in the form `a + bi`
2. **Complex quadratic equations**
3. **Roots of complex numbers** (especially cube roots / fourth roots / higher roots)
4. **Polynomial remainder questions** using the remainder theorem and the form
   `A(z) = B(z)Q(z) + R(z)`
5. **Finding coefficients / roots / factors of complex polynomials**

So the best strategy is:

- first master **1E**
- then do the strongest parts of **1B**
- then the roots part of **1C**
- then the factorisation / root / coefficient parts of **1D**
- leave **1A** for last

# Priority 1 — Absolutely do these

## Oefeningenreeks 1E

These are the most exam-like Chapter 1 exercises of all.

### Must do

- **1E.3–1E.14**

Why these matter:
- They train the exact exam method where you use known remainders at roots of linear factors.
- This is the same template as many old exam Question 2 problems.
- If you only have time for one block, do this one first.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2019–2020, 1st sit, Q1**
- **2021–2022, 1st sit, Q2**
- **2022–2023, 1st sit, Q2**
- **2023–2024, 1st sit, Q2**
- **2024–2025, 2nd sit, Q2**

### Also strongly recommended

- **1E.20–1E.30**
- **1E.31–1E.36**

Why:
- These train “find coefficients so that the polynomial is divisible by …”,
  “find the remaining factor(s)”, and “build a polynomial from root conditions”.
- These are very close to exam questions where you must find a missing coefficient,
  prove divisibility, or recover the other roots.

Typical exam lookalikes:
- **2019–2020, 2nd sit, Q1**
- **2020–2021, 1st sit, Q1**
- **2022–2023, 2nd sit, Q1**
- **2023–2024, 2nd sit, Q2**

### Useful, but after the above

- **1E.16–1E.19**
- **1E.37.1–1E.37.6**

Why:
- These help with real-coefficient polynomials, conjugate roots, and factorisation into quadratic factors.
- They are less direct than 1E.3–14, but still very useful for tougher polynomial questions.

### Note about 1E.5

**1E.5 itself is more of a proof/theorem exercise.** I did **not** find an old exam question that is an exact copy of 1E.5.

But its idea appears everywhere:
- if two divisions by `z-a` and `z-b` give the same remainder,
- then the remainder modulo `(z-a)(z-b)` is that same constant.

So even if 1E.5 is not copied literally, it explains the logic behind many remainder questions.

# Appendix — the key remainder idea behind many of these exercises

A lot of the important exercises above use the same theorem:

```text
A(z) = B(z)Q(z) + R(z)
```

This is just polynomial division with remainder.

- `A(z)` = dividend
- `B(z)` = divisor
- `Q(z)` = quotient
- `R(z)` = remainder

The key rule is:

```text
deg R(z) < deg B(z)
```

So:
- if the divisor has degree 1, the remainder is a constant
- if the divisor has degree 2, the remainder is of the form `az + b`
- if the divisor has degree 3, the remainder is of the form `az^2 + bz + c`

That is exactly why, in a question like the 2024–2025 2nd sit remainder problem, the remainder is taken as a quadratic: the divisor has degree 3.
